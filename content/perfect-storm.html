Understanding Australia's rental crisis

The Australian rental market is experiencing significant strain, with vacancy rates at historic lows. Recent data from SQM Research indicates the national residential vacancy rate fell to just 1.1% in February 2026, down from 1.3%, with Perth recording 0.6% and Brisbane 0.8%. This tightening has driven up costs for tenants, with national advertised rents increasing 6.6% year-on-year and the average reaching $688.76 weekly. Sydney experienced a 7.3% rise over twelve months.

The rental shortage stems from strong population growth, slower new dwelling construction, and increased short-term rental accommodation. For property investors, this environment offers potential for strong rental yields and the opportunity to supply much-needed housing.

Proposed CGT changes: what investors need to know

Property investors currently enjoy a 50% discount on capital gains tax for assets held over 12 months. However, parliamentary inquiries suggest this could reduce to 33% for housing investors. Supporters argue reduced discounts improve affordability and government revenue, while critics warn this could deter investment and worsen rental supply shortages.

The vital role of property investors

Property investors are sometimes unfairly portrayed negatively in public discourse. However, they fulfil an essential function by providing rental housing for millions of Australians, accepting significant financial risks including interest-rate volatility, maintenance costs, unexpected vacancies, and regulatory changes.

Without private investors, governments would shoulder the entire burden of rental provision, which is currently infeasible given the scale of demand. Investors stabilise markets and provide homes for those unable or unwilling to purchase. Success requires rigorous due diligence, comprehensive market analysis, and expert guidance.
