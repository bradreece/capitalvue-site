Every page that ranks for this question was written by someone who wants to lend you money. This one was written by someone who has to tell you, before a search starts, whether you are actually ready.

Between 12 August and 20 October 2026, 68 people completed our Property Readiness Snapshot. Twenty-six of them said they were ready to buy now. Eight of the 68 held a pre-approval.

Those two numbers are the whole article. Ready is a feeling. Pre-approval is the point where it stops being one.

What pre-approval actually is

Pre-approval, conditional approval and approval in principle are the same thing under three names. A lender has looked at your income, your expenses, your debts and your deposit, and has said that on that information it would be prepared to lend you up to a stated amount.

Three words in that sentence carry the weight: on that information. The lender has not seen a property. It has not valued one. It has not confirmed that your circumstances on the day you sign a contract match the ones you described on the day you applied. Pre-approval is a statement about you. Final approval is a statement about you and a specific property together, and it only comes once the valuation and the lender's own checks on that property are done.

That is why a pre-approval is conditional, not binding. A property the lender will not accept, a valuation that comes in under the contract price, or a change in your position between application and contract can all take it away.

The two kinds, and why it matters which one you have

This is the part the lender pages mention in passing and buyers almost never ask about.

Some pre-approvals are system-generated. You enter your figures, an automated assessment runs, and a letter comes back. Nothing has been manually verified. No credit assessor has read a payslip. Lender guidance describes these as less reliable and less comprehensive than the alternative, which is a fully assessed pre-approval, where a person at the lender has gone through your documents and tested the numbers.

A system-generated pre-approval is closer to a quote. A fully assessed one is a position. They can look identical on paper, and the difference only becomes visible at the moment you least want it to, which is after you have signed.

If you are not sure which one you hold, ask your broker or lender one question: was this assessed by a credit assessor, or generated by the system? The answer decides how much weight the letter can carry.

What it isn't

"I've spoken to a broker" is not a position. Of the 68 people in our sample, 25 had spoken to a broker or a bank. Eight held pre-approval. A conversation is the first step toward a position. It is not one.

A borrowing calculator is not a position either, including ours. Our Buying Power Calculator runs a serviceability estimate on the figures you enter, using an average rate and general assumptions about living expenses. It is useful for knowing whether a price range is plausible before you waste a Saturday. It is not an assessment of your file, it has not seen your statements, and no lender is bound by it. The number it gives you is the start of a conversation with a broker, not a substitute for one.

Pre-approval is also not a budget. Our services page puts it as a lender's appetite, not a budget, and that distinction matters just as much once you have the letter in hand. The amount a lender is prepared to advance and the amount you can comfortably hold through a rate rise, a renovation and a change of job are different numbers. Pre-approval tells you the first. Only you can work out the second.

How long it lasts, and what resets it

The range quoted across lender and broker guidance is three to six months, and individual lender certificates commonly run 90 days from issue. Treat the date on the letter as the number that counts.

What resets it before that date:

  • A change in your income or employment, including moving from permanent to contract, or starting probation in a new role
  • A change in your expenses or debts, including a new car loan, a new credit card limit or a buy-now-pay-later account
  • A change in lender policy. Lenders revise serviceability settings, and a pre-approval issued under the old settings may not survive the new ones
  • A change in interest rates. The cash rate target has risen four times in 2026, and each rise lowers the amount the same income will support. A pre-approval written in March describes a market that no longer exists

One more thing the lender pages mention quietly. Each pre-approval application is recorded on your credit file as an enquiry. Several in a short period can lower your score, which then affects the next assessment. Applying to three lenders in a fortnight to see who offers the most is not a free exercise.

The number from people who said they were ready

Eight of 68. Nine of the 68 were buying with cash, and a cash buyer has no need of a pre-approval, so the honest denominator is 59. Eight of those 59 held one. Of the 26 who described themselves as ready now, most did not hold the one document that would let them act on it.

The gap is not carelessness. It is sequencing. People start looking at listings first, because that is the enjoyable part, and finance second, because that is the paperwork. Then the right property appears before the paperwork is done, and the sequence that felt sensible on a Sunday becomes the reason the offer cannot be made on Tuesday.

What it costs at the pointy end is leverage. In the current market, Cotality has capital city homes taking a median of 39 days to sell against 23 a year ago, which means sellers are choosing between fewer offers and weighing each one more carefully. An offer with finance already assessed is a different proposition from an offer with a finance clause and a hope. The buyer with the pre-approval can shorten the finance condition, or drop it. The buyer without one cannot, and the vendor's agent knows exactly which is which.

What to do, in order

The order matters more than the speed.

  1. Documents before broker. Payslips, tax returns or notices of assessment if you are self-employed, three months of statements for every account, and a list of every debt and limit. Having these ready is the difference between a two-day turnaround and a two-week one.
  2. Broker before listings. A broker, or a lender if you prefer to go direct, assesses the file and tells you what a lender will actually support. This is the step that produces the position. Allow a fortnight from first contact to a fully assessed pre-approval, longer if your income is complex.
  3. Listings last. Once you know the number, the search has a boundary, and a boundary is what stops you falling for something you cannot buy.

If you want to know where you stand against that sequence, the Property Readiness Snapshot takes a few minutes and tells you which of finance, brief or timing is the thing holding you up.
