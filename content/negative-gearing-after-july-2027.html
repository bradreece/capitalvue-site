From 1 July 2027, a loss on an established residential property bought after Budget night can no longer reduce the tax on your salary. A loss on a property you already held can, until you sell it. Everything else on this page is detail on those two sentences, and on what changes if you buy through a trust or a company.

The change is law. The Treasury Laws Amendment (Tax Reform No. 1) Act 2026 received Royal Assent on 26 June 2026 and inserts a new section 26-155 into the Income Tax Assessment Act 1997. It applies from the 2027-28 income year. What follows is a description of how the law reads, not advice on what you should do with it.

The date that matters is the contract date, not settlement. A contract signed before 7:30pm on Budget night that settled weeks later is on the grandfathered side of the line. There is no cap on the number of grandfathered properties one investor can hold.

What "quarantined" means

Nothing about which expenses are deductible has changed. Interest, rates, insurance, property management, repairs and depreciation are all still deductions. What changes is where a loss can go once those deductions exceed the rent.

Under the old rules, that loss reduced your taxable income, salary included. Under section 26-155, the excess is not deductible in the year it arises. It becomes a quarantined amount that carries forward and can only be applied against net income from residential dwellings: rent from the same or another residential property, or the capital gain when you sell one. It is deferred, not lost. But it no longer comes back as a larger tax refund each year, and for many investors that annual refund helped make a negatively geared property affordable to hold on a salary.

The practical consequence is about cash, not tax. An investor buying an established property from July 2027 needs to be able to carry the full after-tax shortfall each month, with no refund at the end of the year to recover part of it. That can change how much a lender will be comfortable with, and how much buffer you may need, before anything else about the property is decided. The Buying Power Calculator is a starting point; a broker's assessment is the real number.

New builds: check the definition before you rely on it

The exemption is for a "new residential dwelling", and the definition sits in the Treasury Laws Amendment (Tax Reform No. 3) Bill 2026, introduced on 14 October 2026 and not yet passed. Treasury consulted on the draft definition between 4 and 21 August 2026. The draft treats a dwelling as new where it adds to supply and the investor acquires it within 24 months of a certificate of occupancy being issued. The Budget explainer is clear on the principle: a dwelling built on vacant land, or a knock-down rebuild that increases the number of dwellings, qualifies. A substantial renovation, or a knock-down that replaces one house with one house, does not.

Two consequences follow. The first buyer of a qualifying new dwelling keeps negative gearing, including against salary. Every later buyer of that same dwelling does not, and does not get the old CGT discount either, which may narrow the pool of investors willing to pay a premium for it when you sell. That is a resale question worth asking before you buy new to keep a deduction.

If you buy through a trust

One in three of the purchases we have made for clients has been bought in a trust. For those buyers, the negative gearing change matters less than the headlines suggest, because a trust has never passed a loss to its beneficiaries. A trust's rental loss always stayed in the trust and was carried forward against the trust's own future income. It never reduced anyone's salary.

What does change for trusts is three things. The quarantining rule applies inside the trust too, and it looks through interposed trusts and partnerships to income that comes from residential dwellings, so a trust's residential loss can now only be used against residential income. The 50% CGT discount is replaced for trusts, as it is for individuals, with indexation of the cost base and a 30% minimum tax on real gains accruing from 1 July 2027. And the government has proposed a 30% minimum tax on the taxable income of discretionary trusts from 1 July 2028.

That last measure is not law. The bills were tabled on 14 October 2026, and the ATO's own page describes the measure as "not yet law". Grant Thornton reported on 15 October that the Opposition is not expected to support the bills, so passage depends on the Greens in the Senate. If it passes in its current form, the trustee pays at least 30% on the trust's taxable income, unless a trust in existence at 1 July 2028 elects to make fixed distributions to pre-nominated beneficiaries. Individual beneficiaries get a non-refundable credit for that tax, and a company receiving a distribution gets none. The government has also announced a three-year rollover from 1 July 2027 to move assets out of a discretionary trust into another entity. A federal rollover covers income tax and CGT. It does not cover state stamp duty.

If you buy through a company

Companies are covered by the negative gearing change in the same way, so a company's residential loss is quarantined against its residential income. But like a trust, a company never passed a loss to anyone's salary in the first place, so in practice little changes there.

The bigger shift is relative. Companies never had the 50% CGT discount, so the CGT reform does not touch them, while individuals and trusts lose the discount on gains accruing from July 2027. Add the proposed minimum tax on discretionary trusts, and the gap between holding a property personally, in a trust or in a company narrows. Outside new dwellings and affordable housing, it is the first time since the discount was introduced in 1999 that individuals and trusts will not have it on newly accruing gains. That is one reason some investors are looking at companies again for residential property.

Narrower is not the same as better. Whether a company suits a particular buyer turns on things this change does not touch, and land tax is the clearest example of how much they differ by state. In Queensland, a company or trust pays land tax once its land is worth $350,000 or more, against $600,000 for an individual. In South Australia the 2026-27 general threshold is $936,000 and companies sit on the general scale, while most trusts are assessed from $25,000. The other factors are stamp duty; how money gets out of the company, including Division 7A; and what happens to the property when the shareholders' circumstances change. Commentary on the Act notes that a gain on selling shares in a company or units in a trust that holds a dwelling is not a residential capital gain, so quarantined amounts cannot be used against it. We will cover the company and trust comparison in a separate piece.

If you are thinking about an SMSF

Complying super funds, including SMSFs, are outside the quarantining rule. But the same Act stops super funds, including SMSFs, from using new limited recourse borrowing arrangements, entered from 10 August 2026, to buy real property other than business real property. Existing arrangements are not affected. A fund buying with cash is unaffected by either change.

Moving into or out of your own home

Treasury's draft second tranche proposes that a main residence first used to produce income after the start date keeps the existing negative gearing treatment, so a home you move out of and rent would not be quarantined. It is proposed, not law, and the detail may change before it passes.

What we do not know yet

Three questions come up on calls and are not answered by anything published so far. Whether refinancing or redrawing against a grandfathered property affects its status: nothing we have found says it does, and nothing says it does not. How the rules treat interest on money borrowed against one property and used for another investment: the long-standing rule is that deductibility follows what the money is used for, not what secures it, but how that interacts with section 26-155 and any integrity provisions in the second tranche is a question for a registered tax agent. And what the definition of a new dwelling will be once the No. 3 Bill passes. We will update this page with the date when any of them is settled.

Where this leaves a buyer

If you already hold investment property, the line is drawn. Your grandfathered properties keep their treatment until you sell, which makes the decision to sell one a tax decision as well as a market one.

If you are buying an established property from here, plan on carrying the holding cost without a refund, and get the structure conversation done with your accountant before the contract, not after. Changing the name on a title later usually means paying stamp duty again. Choosing the structure at the start does not.
